# RevenueOS: A Plain-English Guide

Oct 31, 2026

## What RevenueOS is

RevenueOS is a team of AI assistants that does the time-consuming groundwork of sales and marketing, so your people can spend their time talking to customers.

Think of it as hiring a group of very fast, very well-read junior colleagues. One researches companies. One writes first drafts of emails. One takes notes after sales calls. One keeps the customer records tidy. They work in minutes instead of days, and they never get tired.

Like any junior colleague, they show you their work before anything goes out. You stay the decision-maker.

Two things make it different from the AI built into a CRM. It reads **everything your team already knows** about a customer (call recordings, emails, meetings, Slack discussions, the CRM) so every message is specific to that person. And it works with **any CRM**: Salesforce, HubSpot, Microsoft Dynamics 365, Zoho, Pipedrive, or none at all. It can also prove its own value, by comparing leads it contacted with a group it deliberately left alone.

**Who it is for:** salespeople, sales managers, marketers and anyone who talks to prospective customers. You do not need any technical knowledge to use it. If you can use email and a web browser, you can use RevenueOS.

**What it is not:**

- It is not a robot that emails people on its own. Every message waits for a person to approve it.
- It is not a replacement for your judgement. It drafts, suggests and organises; you decide.
- It does not invent customers, results or people. It only uses facts it found or that you gave it, and it warns you when something cannot be checked.

## The golden rule: nothing goes out without you

The assistants can research, write and suggest, but they cannot send an email, post on LinkedIn or publish anything. Everything they write lands in one place, the **Approvals** inbox, and waits there for a person.

In the Approvals inbox you can:

- **Read** each draft and the assistant's reasoning for it.
- **Edit** any word before it goes.
- **Approve** it, which sends the email (or marks a post or message as ready for you to publish).
- **Reject** it, which throws it away.

Why this matters: your company's name is on every message. The assistants are fast and usually right, but a person should always have the final say on what a customer reads.

You don't have to be in RevenueOS to approve. New drafts can also be posted to a Slack channel with **Approve** and **Reject** buttons, and they appear in a RevenueOS panel inside your CRM's record page.

A few things happen automatically, because they protect customers rather than contact them:

- If someone replies "unsubscribe", they are never contacted again.
- If someone replies to an email, any follow-ups still waiting are cancelled, so nobody gets a "just checking in" after they have already answered.
- Follow-up emails you have already approved go out on the day you scheduled them.

## The journey of a customer

At every step from "never heard of us" to "signed", an assistant does the legwork, and a person approves anything the customer will see.

The journey runs in ten steps: a buying signal is spotted, the lead is scored, the company and person are researched, emails are drafted, **a person approves them** (the one step only a person can do), replies are triaged, the meeting is prepped, the call is analysed, a proposal is built, and the deal is reviewed until it is signed.

Later emails wait for your approval too: the reply drafted at step 6 and the thank-you email after the call at step 8. Proposals are files you download, check and send yourself.

**An example.** The Signal Detector notices that a logistics company has just hired a new Head of Data. The Lead Scorer rates her contact an A, the Researcher writes a brief about the company's plans, and the Writer drafts three short emails that mention her new role. You tweak one sentence and approve. She replies "interested"; Reply Triage drafts an answer offering two meeting times. Before the call you read the Meeting Prep brief; afterwards you paste your notes and the Call Analyst creates the deal and a thank-you email. A week later the Proposal Builder produces the proposal and deck, and the Deal Strategist suggests bringing in her finance director to get it signed.

## Everything your team knows, in one place

Each customer has a **Customer timeline** (on their page) that collects every conversation the team has had with them, and every assistant reads it before writing or advising. That is how a follow-up can say "as you mentioned on our call last Tuesday" and be right.

| Where it comes from | What happens, without anyone lifting a finger |
| --- | --- |
| Your email (Gmail or Outlook) | Replies from customers pause their emails and get an answer drafted; emails you send yourself are noted. Emails with people who aren't customers are never stored |
| Your calendar (Google or Outlook) | Customer meetings are spotted, and a prep brief is ready a day before |
| Call recordings (Zoom, Teams, Google Meet, Gong) | After the call, the recording's transcript is summarised, the deal is updated and a thank-you email is drafted |
| Slack | Deal discussions in the channels your team chooses are summarised as notes |
| Your CRM | New leads and changes to deals made in the CRM come in every 15 minutes |
| You | Type a note on the timeline at any time |

**Internal stays internal.** Notes from internal Slack channels, and notes you mark "internal only", help the assistants decide what to say, but they are never quoted to the customer. A draft that repeats internal wording is blocked.

Each person connects their own email and calendar once, on the **Integrations** page. Access is read-only.

## Everyday tasks, step by step

Most tasks take two or three clicks; the assistants then work for one to three minutes while you do something else.

### Add a new lead

1. Open **Pipeline**.
2. Scroll to **Add a lead**, fill in first name, last name and company (the rest is optional but helps).
3. Click **Add lead**. To add many at once, use **Import contacts CSV** on the Market intel page with a spreadsheet saved as CSV.

### Get a personal email sequence written

1. Open **Pipeline** and find the person.
2. Click **Research**. Wait until it finishes; a "brief ready" tag appears next to the company.
3. Click **Write sequence**. Three emails are drafted: a first email and two follow-ups.
4. Go to **Approvals** to review them.

Tip: to do many people at once, tick the boxes next to their names and click **Run outbound on selected**. Poor-fit leads are skipped automatically.

### Approve or reject drafts

1. Open **Approvals** and type your name in the box at the top (only needed once).
2. Read each draft and the yellow or red notes under it.
3. Change any wording directly in the box.
4. Click **Approve & send** (or **Approve & schedule** for follow-ups), or **Reject**.

### Prepare for a sales call

1. Click the person's name in **Pipeline**.
2. Click **Prep a meeting**, add when it is, who will attend and your goal.
3. Click **Write prep brief**. The brief appears under **Documents** on the same page.

### Record what happened on a call

1. On the person's page, click **Analyse a call**.
2. Paste the call transcript, or type your notes in plain sentences.
3. Click **Analyse call**. The deal is created or updated on the **Deals** page, and a thank-you email waits in **Approvals**.

If your calls are recorded on Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet or Gong and those are connected, this happens automatically after the call; you only check the thank-you email in Approvals.

### Create a proposal and pitch deck

1. On the person's page, click **Build proposal + deck**.
2. Optionally type guidance, for example "focus on the quick assessment, keep it to two phases".
3. Click **Build**. Download the Word and PowerPoint files from **Documents** and edit them as you like.

### Plan a marketing campaign

1. Open **Campaigns**.
2. Describe the goal in a sentence, add a budget and dates if you know them, and click **Plan campaign**.
3. Open the plan, check it, then click **Draft all content** (or **Draft** next to one item).
4. Review the pieces in **Approvals**, using the "marketing content" filter.

### Make an email more personal

1. On the person's page, click **Research person**. A short profile with links to their public talks, interviews and articles appears on the page.
2. Click **Write sequence** (on the Pipeline page). The writer now uses the profile and the customer timeline.
3. In **Approvals**, check the **Personalisation** score (80+ is very specific; under 60 means it was sent back once and is still generic) and the **Sources** listed under the draft.

### Reach everyone at an important company

1. On any contact's page at that company, click **Committee outreach**.
2. RevenueOS plans one shared story, then writes a different sequence for each person (for example the champion, the finance director, the technical lead), spaced out so they don't all arrive on the same day.
3. It also lists roles nobody at the company covers yet, so you know who to find.

### Tell the assistants something they should know

1. On the person's page, type in the box under **Customer timeline**, e.g. "Priya prefers short emails".
2. Leave **internal only** ticked if the customer must never see it, then click **Add note**.

### Approve from Slack

1. New drafts appear in your team's approvals channel.
2. Click **Approve & send** or **Reject**; to change wording, click **Edit in RevenueOS**.
3. In any channel, type `/revenueos` followed by a request, e.g. `/revenueos brief me on Siemens before my 3pm`. The answer comes back privately.

### Run a blind test

1. Open **Blind test** and give the test a name.
2. For leads that already have a RevenueOS draft, paste the email your team (or another tool) would have sent.
3. Ask colleagues to click **Rate**: they see two emails, A and B, and pick the better one without knowing which RevenueOS wrote. Click **Results** to see the verdict.

### Start a pilot

1. Open **Setup checklist** and work down the steps.
2. At step 6, click **Start the pilot**. RevenueOS writes outreach for up to 50 new leads and randomly holds back about 1 in 5 as a comparison group that gets nothing.

## Talking to the Copilot

Write to the Copilot the way you would brief a capable colleague: say who, what and why, in ordinary sentences. It decides which assistants to use and tells you what it did and what to do next.

**Requests that work well:**

- "Score all new leads and tell me which three to call this week."
- "Research Siemens and write a three-email sequence for their Chief Data Officer, with two versions of the first email."
- "I just had a call with Priya. Here are my notes: … Update the deal and draft the follow-up."
- "Prepare me for Tuesday's meeting with Maersk. Elena and her CFO will attend; I want to agree a pilot."
- "Build a proposal for Crocs focused on fixing their CRM data."
- "Review all open deals and tell me which ones are at risk."
- "What is our forecast for this quarter against a target of 500,000?"
- "Plan a campaign to get 30 meetings with logistics IT leaders in Europe this quarter."
- "Write a battlecard on our biggest competitor."
- "Find 10 mid-size manufacturers that are investing in data and AI."

**Tips:**

- Be specific about the person, company and goal; the more context, the better the result.
- It is fine to ask follow-up questions; the Copilot remembers its last answer.
- Bigger requests take a few minutes. The line under the answer shows which assistants worked on it and what it cost.
- Anything it writes for customers still goes to **Approvals** first.

**Newer things you can ask for:**

- "Research Priya at Siemens as a person, then write her a sequence."
- "Plan outreach to everyone we know at Maersk, one story, different angle for each person."
- "Is our AI outreach working? Show me the control-group results."
- "What buying signals came up this week?"

You can also ask from Slack: type `/revenueos` and your request in any channel.

## Teaching it about your company

The assistants are only as good as what they know about you, and everything they know comes from the **Knowledge base** page. Ten minutes spent there improves every email, proposal and campaign.

The assistants are allowed to quote facts about your company only if they appear here. If a draft mentions a number that is not in the knowledge base or the research, a yellow warning appears so you can check it before sending.

| Type of document | What to put in it | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Offering | What you sell and the results it brings | "Our 4-week data assessment finds the top 5 AI opportunities" |
| Case study | Real customer stories with real, approved numbers | "Forecast accuracy rose from 61% to 84% for a manufacturer" |
| Ideal customer profile | The kind of company you want to sell to | Industry, size, country, signs they are ready to buy |
| Persona | The people who buy, and what each cares about | "CFOs care about cost and risk" |
| Brand voice | How your company writes | "Short sentences, no jargon, never pushy" |
| Rules | Hard rules every draft must follow, one per line | `BANNED: synergy` blocks any draft using it; `REQUIRED: Results vary by client` warns if missing; `TONE: Never use exclamation marks` is followed by every writer |
| Pricing | What salespeople may and may not say about price | "Never quote prices in a first email" |
| Objection | Common push-back and the approved answer | "We already have a partner" and how to respond |
| Competitor | How you compare with rivals | Filled in automatically by Competitor Intel too |
| Playbook | What has worked in your own outreach | Written automatically by the Learning assistant |

**How to add something:** open **Knowledge base**, type a title, pick the type, paste the text, and click **Save document**. You can also upload a .txt or .md file.

**Before you go live:** the system arrives with sample documents marked "SAMPLE CONTENT" and four made-up sample leads. The quickest way to replace them is step 3 of the **Setup checklist**: paste what you sell, your ideal customer, one approved customer result and your writing style, and tick **Remove the sample documents**. Only include case studies your company has approved for public use.

## Safety, privacy and cost

RevenueOS checks every draft automatically before you see it, protects people who opt out, and records what every assistant cost.

### What the warnings under a draft mean

| Warning | Colour | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Figures not found in the knowledge base or brief | Yellow | Check the number is true; delete it if you are not sure |
| Sources couldn't be verified / Draft states figures but lists no sources | Yellow | Check the claim against the source listed under the draft |
| Personalisation below the target after a rewrite | Yellow | Add something specific to the person, or reject it |
| Missing required wording | Yellow | Add the wording your rules require, e.g. a disclaimer |
| Spam-trigger phrases | Yellow | Reword phrases like "act now" or "guaranteed" so the email is not flagged as spam |
| Too long | Yellow | Shorten it; first emails work best under about 150 words |
| Unfilled placeholder, e.g. \[First Name\] | Red | Replace it with the real word; it cannot be sent until you do |
| Repeats internal team discussion | Red | Rephrase it: the wording came from an internal Slack channel or note |
| Mentions the person's family, health, home, beliefs or age | Red | Remove it; outreach stays on professional topics |
| Uses phrases your rules ban | Red | Remove the banned phrase |
| Recipient is on the suppression list | Red | This person asked not to be contacted; it cannot be sent |
| Contact has no email address | Red | Add the email address to the lead first |

### Protecting the people you contact

- Every email automatically includes your company address and an easy way to unsubscribe, as email laws require.
- Anyone who unsubscribes or replies "remove me" is blocked from all future emails.
- There are daily limits on how many emails go out in total and to any one company, which protects your email reputation.
- LinkedIn messages are never sent by the system; you copy and send them yourself, as LinkedIn's rules require.
- The Lead Sourcer finds companies only. It never invents people's names or email addresses.

### Privacy

- Your data stays in your company's own database. The assistants send only the text needed for each task to the AI service that powers them, Claude by Anthropic.
- When RevenueOS serves several clients, each client has a **completely separate database**, so one client's information can never appear in another's.
- Everyone signs in with their own account. Admins manage who has access under **Settings**.
- Passwords and connection keys are stored encrypted and are never shown again after they are saved.
- Email and calendar connections are read-only, and only emails with known customers are kept.
- Slack is read only in the channels your team chooses.
- The full text of calls, emails and Slack messages is deleted after 90 days; the short summaries are kept.

### Cost

- Each task an assistant does uses some paid AI capacity. Quick jobs like scoring a lead cost the least; web research, proposals and deal reviews cost the most.
- The **Dashboard** shows total AI spend and spend per assistant, and **Insights** shows the cost per meeting booked.
- On **AI governance**, admins can set a monthly budget for all AI spend and for each assistant. When a budget is reached, that work stops until the budget is raised, and a warning goes to Slack at 80%.
- Admins can also choose a cheaper or more capable AI model per assistant, or switch an assistant off.
- Automatic background jobs (scanning for buying signals, weekly reviews) are switched off unless an admin turns them on.

## Proving it works

RevenueOS doesn't ask you to take its value on trust: it measures it with your own numbers, on the **Insights** page.

| Question | How RevenueOS answers it |
| --- | --- |
| Does AI outreach actually add meetings? | **Control group:** when outreach runs on a batch of leads, about 1 in 5 are picked at random and left alone. Comparing the two groups shows what the outreach added. A result is declared only once each group has 30+ people and the difference is too big to be luck |
| Are its emails better than ours? | **Blind test:** colleagues compare a RevenueOS email with your team's email for the same lead, without knowing which is which |
| Does making emails more personal pay off? | Reply rates are shown for emails with low, medium and high personalisation scores |
| Which subject line or angle works better? | **A/B tests:** when asked, two versions of the first email are written and each person randomly gets one |
| Is it worth the money? | **Return on AI spend:** AI cost, cost per meeting, and the pipeline and won deals at companies RevenueOS emailed before the deal started |

Click **Download the pilot report** on Insights for all of this in one document to share with management.

Leads in the control group are protected: no assistant will write to them unless someone clicks **Release** on their page (which also takes them out of the results).

## First-time setup

Installing RevenueOS is a job for your IT team or a technical colleague; the full instructions are in the README file in the RevenueOS folder (and on GitHub). After that, almost everything is set up inside the app, on the **Setup checklist** page.

**Hand this list to IT (one-off, on the server):**

- [ ] Put the Anthropic API key in the settings file (`.env`). Without it, nothing can be researched or written.
- [ ] Keep the generated `SECRET_KEY` in that file private; it protects every stored password and connection key.
- [ ] Email sending details (SMTP). Until set, approved emails are only logged, which is a safe way to try it out.
- [ ] If you want email/calendar sync: a Google and/or Microsoft app registration (RevenueOS shows the exact web addresses to register).
- [ ] If you want Slack: a Slack app, its signing secret and bot token, and an approvals channel.
- [ ] If you use Zoom or Gong for calls: their webhook secret or API key.
- [ ] Optionally, the RevenueOS panel installed inside your CRM (templates are in the `crm-panels` folder).

**Your part, in the app (Setup checklist):**

- [ ] Create the owner account on the sign-in page. From then on everyone needs an account; add colleagues under **Settings**.
- [ ] Company details: name, registered address (it appears in every email), the address emails come from.
- [ ] Connect your CRM (Salesforce, HubSpot, Dynamics 365, Zoho, Pipedrive, or none) and click **Test connection**. Use a test ("sandbox") account first.
- [ ] Teach it about your company and remove the sample content.
- [ ] Bring in leads: import from the CRM, upload a spreadsheet, or add them by hand.
- [ ] Connect your email and calendar on **Integrations** (each person does their own).
- [ ] Start the pilot with 2 to 4 people for four weeks, then download the pilot report from **Insights**.

**Serving several clients?** The platform owner creates a separate workspace for each client under **Client workspaces**. Each has its own data, settings, CRM and people, and its own Setup checklist.

## Questions and troubleshooting

| Question or problem | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| I clicked a button and nothing seems to happen | The assistants are working; most jobs take 1 to 3 minutes, campaigns and proposals up to 5. Stay on the page and the result appears when ready. |
| I'm sent to the sign-in page | Your session ended (they last 14 days) or you signed out. Sign in again; if you have no account, ask your admin. |
| I approved an email. Did it really go out? | Look at the top of the **Dashboard**: "Email: dry\_run" means emails are only logged for testing, "smtp" means they are really sent. |
| A draft has a red box and won't send | Fix what the red box says (a placeholder, banned phrase, internal wording, personal detail or missing email address), then click Approve again. |
| The email mentions a number I don't recognise | That is what the yellow warning is for. Delete the number or check it, and add true figures to the knowledge base so they can be used next time. |
| The writing sounds generic | Click **Research** and **Research person** first, connect email and call recordings so the timeline has real conversations, and make sure the knowledge base has your offering, case studies and brand voice. |
| A step failed with "Could not resolve authentication method" | The Anthropic API key is missing or wrong. Ask IT to check the settings file. |
| A step failed with "budget reached" | The monthly AI budget for that assistant (or overall) is used up. An admin can raise it on **AI governance**. |
| A prospect replied but I can't see it | Replies are picked up automatically once your mailbox is connected on **Integrations** (checked every 15 minutes). |
| A call didn't show up on the timeline | If RevenueOS couldn't tell who the call was with, it waits under "Needs a home" on **Integrations**: pick the company and person. |
| Why can't I write to this lead? | They are in the control group for the pilot. Click **Release** on their page if you really need to contact them. |
| Why do I see drafts marked "A/B test: randomly assigned the other variant"? | When you ask for two versions of an email, each person randomly gets one; the other is set aside automatically. |
| Why is the learned playbook empty? | It needs at least 20 sent emails and 3 replies before it can learn anything reliable. |
| Can it book meetings in my calendar? | Not yet. It drafts replies that suggest times; you book the meeting yourself. |
| Does it update our CRM? | Yes, once connected: it logs emails and calls, updates lead status, and creates or updates deals in Salesforce, HubSpot, Dynamics 365, Zoho or Pipedrive. |
| Can I use it from another AI assistant? | Yes. IT can connect RevenueOS to Claude or other assistants that support MCP, a standard way for AI assistants to use other tools. |

## Glossary

| Word | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **AI assistant (agent)** | A program that does one job, like researching or writing, by reading and reasoning like a person would. |
| **AI governance** | The page where admins set a monthly spending limit for each assistant (and overall) and choose which AI model each one uses. |
| **Approval queue** | The list of drafts waiting for a person to say yes or no before anything goes out. |
| **Blind test** | A fair comparison: reps see two emails for the same lead without knowing which one RevenueOS wrote, and pick the better one. |
| **Budget** | The most money the assistants may spend on AI in a month. When it is reached, they stop until an admin raises it or the month ends. |
| **Buying committee** | All the people at a company who have a say in a purchase, such as the person who signs, the person who will use it and the finance lead. |
| **Control group (holdout)** | A small, randomly chosen share of leads that RevenueOS deliberately does not contact, so you can compare results and see the real difference it makes. |
| **CRM** | The system your company uses to keep track of customers and deals, such as Salesforce, HubSpot, Dynamics 365, Zoho or Pipedrive. RevenueOS works with any of these, or on its own. |
| **Customer timeline** | Everything your team knows about one company in date order: emails, replies, calls, Slack discussions and meetings, each with a short summary. |
| **Deal** | A possible sale being worked on, with a value and a stage, such as "proposal sent". |
| **Knowledge base** | The documents you give RevenueOS about your company: what you sell, who you sell to, success stories, pricing and how you like to write. |
| **Lead** | A person who might become a customer. |
| **Person profile (dossier)** | What RevenueOS found in public, professional sources about one person: their role, things they have written or said, and their priorities, each with a link to where it came from. |
| **Personalisation score** | A mark from 0 to 100 for how specific a draft is to this person and company. Low-scoring drafts are rewritten once before you see them. |
